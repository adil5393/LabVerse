## What the textbook actually does (and how that shaped the derivation)

- Section 6.5 builds the law **empirically**: Activities 6.3 and 6.4 (a cart pulled by a falling cup, same distance from rest, `s = ½aT²`, so `a₁/a₂ = T₂²/T₁²`) show a ∝ F for a fixed mass and a ∝ 1/m for a fixed force; Eq. 6.1/6.2 `a = F/m`, `F = ma`; the newton is **defined** as the force giving 1 kg an acceleration of 1 m/s² (1 N = 1 kg m/s²); Eq. 6.3 weight `F = mg` (9.8 m/s², 10 for estimates). Examples 6.4 (barbell), 6.5 (block with friction), 6.6 (car velocity-time graph); Pause and Ponder 6 to 8.
- **Momentum is not derived in the textbook.** "Ready to Go Beyond" only defines momentum (mass × velocity, direction of the velocity) and states the rate-of-change-of-momentum form (valid also for variable mass); "Bridging Science and Society" gives the cricket catch, airbag and coconut.
- Therefore two derivations are provided and labelled honestly: **A, the textbook's own argument** (experimental, ending by choosing the unit so the constant is 1; the page says it is not a mathematical proof) and **B, from momentum, which is LabVerse enrichment** (`p = mv`, `Δp = m(v − u)`, `Δp/Δt = ma`, `F = Δp/Δt = ma`).
